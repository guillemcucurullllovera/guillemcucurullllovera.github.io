Quantum & quantitative research — live, vs classical

research & engineering running live

A production quantum machine-learning layer inside the live trading engine — across FX, options, futures and crypto — where four quantum models run as an ensemble with, and benchmarked against, tuned classical baselines, on simulators and on real IBM Quantum hardware.

VQC — directional classifier

A 14-qubit variational quantum classifier (data re-uploading, domain-aware entanglement, parameter-shift gradients) producing calibrated up/down probabilities per asset from a 14-feature vector.

QAOA — portfolio optimizer

Cardinality-constrained allocation as a QUBO solved with QAOA, using a constraint-preserving XY-mixer + Dicke-state initialization that stays inside the feasible K-hot manifold.

Quantum-kernel anomaly (qrbf-OCSVM)

A one-class SVM whose RBF kernel is replaced by a fidelity quantum kernel — market-state similarity computed in a Hilbert space no classical kernel reaches — for distribution-free regime / crisis alarms.

Quantum Reservoir Computing

A transverse-field Ising reservoir as a fixed quantum feature extractor with a light classical readout — quantum temporal richness with no barren-plateau risk.

Ensemble & validation

The quantum models combine with classical signals in a Bayesian gating ensemble weighted by regime-conditional out-of-sample reliability, on identical walk-forward folds with purged labels and modelled transaction costs — the quantum layer adding decorrelated, non-linear signal that lifts the ensemble beyond its classical-only configuration.

IBM Quantum hardware

Circuits run on PennyLane-Lightning and Qiskit at machine-precision parity, with validation and advantage runs on real IBM Quantum (Eagle / Heron) via Runtime primitives — full NISQ mitigation: ZNE, dynamical decoupling, Pauli twirling, SABRE transpilation.

Frontier

Migrating to error-corrected logical qubits on IBM's fault-tolerant roadmap, quantum amplitude estimation for Monte-Carlo CVaR (quadratic speed-up), and quantum-enhanced reinforcement learning for execution.

Operator-theoretic forecasting

Alongside, a GEDMD / Koopman-operator forecaster stabilized by multiscale Koopman-consistency, chaos diagnostics, a Monte-Carlo tail-risk veto and split-conformal intervals — fused into a Composite Stability Index, extended from FX to options vol surfaces.

Detector calibration at CERN / PSI — CMS Phase-2 upgrade

data engineering & experimental physics

At PSI I characterized and calibrated next-generation RD53B / CROC pixel readout chips on prototype modules for the CMS Phase-2 Inner Tracker at the High-Luminosity LHC — the detector-grade rigor I now bring to AI, quant and data systems.

🔗

Publicly listed by the Paul Scherrer Institute's High Energy Physics group: "Guillem Cucurull Llovera (Semester thesis, Physics, University of Zurich, July 2023): Calibration and Testing of Prototype Modules for the CMS Phase-2 Pixel detector upgrade." Supervised by Prof. Dr. Lea Caminada.   Verify on psi.ch ↗

The work

At PSI I characterized and calibrated next-generation RD53B / CROC pixel readout chips (65 nm CMOS) on prototype modules for the CMS Phase-2 Inner Tracker upgrade at the High-Luminosity LHC — bringing up the test hardware and running the full calibration chain on real detector modules.

Techniques

Operated the Ph2_ACF / CMSITminiDAQ + FC7-FPGA readout stack inside a temperature-controlled coldbox with an X-ray source; ran PixelAlive, noise, S-curve, gain (ToT), per-pixel threshold equalization and global threshold-adjustment scans, plus IV / voltage tuning via a custom probe card.

Results

Tuned per-pixel thresholds to ~2,000 e⁻ and equalized gain across chips; reduced noisy / masked pixels from ~1,200–1,300 per chip to 0–2 per chip after calibration, and identified the working on-module data-merging configuration (diagnosing defective chips and disconnected bump bonds).

Output

Documented the methodology in a supervised report and presented the calibration findings to the CMS group. This detector-grade rigor — reproducibility, monitoring, quantified results — is what I now bring to AI, quant and data systems.

How I got there

Continuous graduate research and coursework at the University of Zürich (2022–2024) built the toolkit the program demanded — C / C++ and Python for performance-critical data processing, parallel / HPC computing, numerical methods and reproducible scientific workflows. Within it, I spent ~7 months in 2023 on the PSI / CERN detector-calibration work itself, productive on the test bench from day one.

What it demanded (and proved)

Bring-up of a full data-acquisition chain (Ph2_ACF / CMSITminiDAQ on an FC7 FPGA), low-level chip configuration over registers and DACs, error-function fitting of threshold / noise S-curves, per-pixel trimming, and disciplined, reproducible measurement under real lab constraints — the same signal-from-noise rigor I now bring to ML, quant and trading systems.

Foundations

BSc Physics (minor in Mathematics), Boston University — top of class in Quantum Mechanics I & II and a master's-level Advanced Electronics Laboratory under Prof. Lawrence R. Sulak, where the experimental-electronics skillset behind this detector work began.
